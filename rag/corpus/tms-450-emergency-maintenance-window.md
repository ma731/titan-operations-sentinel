# TMS-450: Emergency Maintenance Window Procedure

## S1 When an emergency window may be opened

An emergency maintenance window may be opened when a machine is in the Critical or Trip
severity band under TMS-101, and the next scheduled window falls outside the predicted
failure window. It is not opened to recover schedule, to accommodate a late parts
delivery, or to avoid a weekend premium.

The decision to open a window is a plant manager decision. A reliability assessment
recommends it. It does not authorise it.

## S2 Prerequisites before the window opens

All of the following must be in place before the window opens:

1. A parts plan with confirmed availability for the full bill of materials, including the
   arrival time of any expedited part.
2. Named technicians with the required certifications, confirmed available for the full
   duration.
3. A production plan covering the jobs displaced from the machine, checked for operator
   and robot cell conflicts.
4. A lockout/tagout plan and an assigned authorised employee, per `osha-1910-147-loto`.
5. A safety officer sign-off recorded against the intervention.
6. A quality containment plan for product made in the suspect window, per
   `iatf-16949-traceability-records`.

A window opened without a confirmed parts arrival time is the single most common cause of
an overrun. Do not open on an estimated delivery.

## S3 Weekend and off-shift windows

A weekend window is preferred where the predicted failure window allows it, because the
production displacement cost is lowest and the technician availability is highest. A
Saturday window on the Leipzig site carries a premium labour rate and requires 24 hours
notice to the works council.

A window that starts less than 12 hours before the lower bound of the predicted failure
window is treated as a same-shift emergency and requires the plant manager to be present
or reachable for the duration.

## S4 Duration and overrun

The planned duration is the estimated repair hours from the asset profile plus the
isolation and requalification time, which is 2 hours for a machining centre. An overrun
beyond 150 percent of the planned duration is escalated to the plant manager, who decides
between continuing and restoring the machine to a safe idle state.

## S5 Requalification before release

A machine is not released back to production on the completion of the mechanical work. It
is released on a successful requalification: a warm-up cycle, a condition monitoring
check showing the machine has returned to the Normal band, and a first article inspection
that passes. All three are recorded.

## S6 Cost authority

Spend arising inside an emergency window follows the standard authority ladder. Routine
consumables and any spend at or below the autonomous ceiling proceed without a specific
approval. Spend above the ceiling, including expedited freight and premium labour,
requires the plant manager approval on the record before the commitment is made, not
after.
