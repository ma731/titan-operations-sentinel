# OSHA 1910.147: Control of Hazardous Energy (Lockout/Tagout)

> Paraphrase note: a plain-language summary of the lockout/tagout rule, written for
> retrieval. It is not the regulatory text. Cite 29 CFR 1910.147 itself for any
> compliance determination, and follow the plant energy control procedure.

## S1 What the rule covers

The rule covers the servicing and maintenance of machines where the unexpected start-up
of the machine, or the release of stored energy, could injure the person doing the work.
It applies whenever an employee must remove or bypass a guard, or place any part of their
body into a point of operation or a danger zone.

It does not apply to minor tool changes and adjustments that are routine, repetitive and
integral to production, provided the work is done using alternative measures that give
effective protection.

## S2 Energy sources in scope

Hazardous energy is not only electrical. The rule covers electrical, mechanical,
hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical, thermal and stored energy such as gravity, springs, and
capacitance. A spindle intervention on a CNC machining centre typically involves at
minimum electrical isolation, hydraulic isolation of the drawbar circuit, pneumatic
isolation, and the residual rotational energy of the spindle itself.

## S3 The energy control procedure

The employer must have documented, machine-specific energy control procedures. The
required sequence is:

1. Notify affected employees that the machine is being shut down.
2. Shut the machine down using its normal stopping procedure.
3. Isolate every energy source using the energy isolating devices.
4. Apply a lock and a tag to each isolating device. Each authorised employee applies
   their own lock.
5. Release, restrain, or otherwise render safe all stored and residual energy.
6. Verify isolation by attempting to start the machine, then returning controls to off.

Only after step 6 may the servicing begin.

## S4 Who may apply and remove a lock

Only an authorised employee, trained for that machine and that procedure, may apply or
remove a lock. A lock is removed by the person who applied it. Removal by anyone else is
permitted only under a documented exception procedure that includes verifying the owner
is not at the facility, making all reasonable efforts to contact them, and ensuring they
are informed before they resume work.

No agent, automated system, or supervisor may authorise the removal of a lock. Any
proposed action that would disable, bypass, or modify a safety interlock, a guard, or an
energy isolating device is outside the authority of any automated system and must be
halted and referred to the safety officer.

## S5 Group lockout and shift handover

When more than one person services a machine, a group lockout device is used so that each
person applies a personal lock, and the machine cannot be re-energised until the last
lock is removed. When servicing continues across a shift change, the outgoing and
incoming authorised employees must transfer locks so continuity of protection is never
broken.

## S6 Training and records

Authorised employees are trained in recognising hazardous energy sources and in the
isolation methods. Affected employees are trained in the purpose and use of the
procedure. The employer must inspect each energy control procedure at least annually and
certify the inspection, recording the machine, the date, the employees involved, and the
inspector.

Every lockout event produces a lockout/tagout reference that belongs in the maintenance
record for the intervention.

## S7 Interaction with emergency maintenance

An emergency does not remove the lockout/tagout requirement. Time pressure from a
production loss or a predicted machine failure is not a basis for skipping isolation. An
emergency maintenance window is planned so that the isolation procedure fits inside it,
not so it can be omitted.
