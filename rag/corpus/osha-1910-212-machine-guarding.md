# OSHA 1910.212: General Requirements for All Machines (Guarding)

> Paraphrase note: a plain-language summary written for retrieval. Not the regulatory
> text. Cite 29 CFR 1910.212 for any compliance determination.

## S1 The general duty to guard

One or more methods of machine guarding must be provided to protect the operator and
other employees in the machine area from hazards created by the point of operation,
ingoing nip points, rotating parts, flying chips and sparks. Guarding is the employer
duty, not an operator option.

## S2 Acceptable guarding methods

Acceptable methods include fixed barrier guards, interlocked guards, two-hand tripping
devices, presence-sensing devices such as light curtains and safety mats, and electronic
safety devices. The guard must prevent the operator from having any part of their body in
the danger zone during the operating cycle.

The guard must itself not create a hazard: it must not introduce a pinch point, and it
must be secured so it cannot be removed casually.

## S3 Point of operation guarding

Where a machine has a point of operation that exposes an employee to injury, the point of
operation must be guarded. The guarding device must conform to any applicable standard,
and where none exists it must prevent the operator from having any part of their body in
the danger zone during the operating cycle.

For a CNC machining centre this means the enclosure doors are interlocked with the
spindle drive, and the machine will not run at cutting speed with a door open.

## S4 Interlock integrity

An interlocked guard must stop the hazardous motion when the guard is opened, and must
not allow the hazardous motion to restart simply by closing the guard. Restart requires a
deliberate action at the control.

Defeating, bypassing, jumpering or taping an interlock is prohibited. Any proposal to
run a machine with an interlock bypassed, including for diagnostic purposes, requires a
documented safety officer authorisation and a written temporary safeguarding plan. No
automated system may grant that authorisation.

## S5 Anchoring and stationary machines

Machines designed for a fixed location must be securely anchored to prevent walking or
moving. This applies to machining centres, presses, and grinders.

## S6 Inspection and defect reporting

Guards and interlocks are inspected on the plant preventive maintenance schedule. A guard
found damaged, missing, or defeated takes the machine out of service until it is restored.
An interlock that has cycled unexpectedly, for example during a shift handover, is logged
and investigated before the next production shift, even when the machine reports a
nominal state.
